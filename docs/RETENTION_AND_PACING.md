# RETENTION_AND_PACING.md

> Added 2026-05-29 from Jaime's notes. Two questions this doc answers:
> (1) How do we keep turns from feeling slow? (2) How do we give players honest reasons to
> return — without the manipulative tricks the genre is infamous for? Honesty is our
> differentiator, so the "what we refuse to do" list is as important as the hooks.

## Pacing — a week is a SIM unit, not a decision rate
- **Calm weeks fly by, dramatic weeks slow down.** When nothing interesting is happening, the
  player taps through multiple weeks quickly (auto-resolve routine income/expenses). When an
  **event, big choice, or crisis** hits, the game stops and demands a decision. Decision density
  stays high; dead time stays low.
- **No forced waiting.** A turn resolves instantly on tap. No real-time energy timers gating
  progress (see refusals below).
- **Time compression unlocks with wealth (Phase 2):** as the player stabilizes, allow
  weekly→monthly→quarterly turn modes so the late game (running businesses, investing) doesn't
  drag through trivial weeks. Big moments still interrupt.
- **Session shape goal:** a satisfying sitting = several weeks advanced + 1–3 meaningful
  decisions + visible progress toward a milestone.

## The "always a next pull" principle (open-ended progression)
The game never ends, so retention comes from *a goal always being visible just ahead*:
- **Milestone ladder always shows the next rung** (Stability → Comfort → Business owner →
  Investor → Wealthy → ...). The player can always see what they're climbing toward.
- **"What happens next" hooks:** ongoing storylines/conditions (a business you started, a debt
  you're clearing, a relationship, a risky bet pending) that resolve in future turns and pull
  the player back to see the outcome.
- **New scenarios + replay:** different starting lives are their own reason to return ("try it
  as the immigrant / single parent / heir"). Cheap to add, high replay value.
- **Beat-your-own-run:** personal bests (fastest to stability, highest net worth, most
  businesses) give self-competition without needing multiplayer.

## Optional async / between-session advancement (designed, light in v1)
- Some slow processes (a business accruing revenue, an investment maturing, a course finishing)
  can **advance while the player is away**, so returning is rewarding ("your shop earned money;
  your course finished — what next?"). Opt-in feel, not guilt-driven.
- v1: keep this minimal (maybe one "your side gig earned X while you were gone" on return).
  Deepen in Phase 2 with businesses/investments.

## Honest re-engagement (what we DO)
- Show real progress and the next reachable goal.
- Resolve pending storylines the player chose to start.
- Offer fresh scenarios/content.
- Optional, respectful notifications: "Your business has news" — clearly useful, easy to turn
  off, never spammy.

## What we REFUSE to do (the trust line)
- ❌ Real-time energy/stamina timers that block play until you wait or pay.
- ❌ FOMO/limited-time pressure designed to create anxiety.
- ❌ Daily-login guilt streaks that punish missing a day.
- ❌ Manipulative push-notification spam.
- ❌ Pay-to-skip-a-wall-we-artificially-built. (We don't build artificial walls.)
> Rule: a retention mechanic must come from the player *wanting* to see what's next, not from us
> withholding or pressuring. If it only works by manufacturing anxiety, it's out.

## Metrics this doc is accountable to (v1)
- Turns per session, session length, sessions per user per week.
- D1 / D7 / D30 return rate.
- Restart rate + new-scenario tries (curiosity/replay signal).
- Drop-off points: where in a run do people stop? (tune pacing/events there.)
